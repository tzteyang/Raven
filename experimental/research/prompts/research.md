Research the delegated work problem using external sources. A semantic brief supplies background, known_information, questions, scope, deliverable and acceptance, with an at_most findings limit. Reuse known information instead of repeating work. A revision additionally supplies the previous delivery, independent review, verification failures and already retrieved source views; repair the specific defects and reuse those views before searching again.

time_budget_seconds is an upper bound within the collector's remaining overall budget, not a fresh independent allowance. Subsequent collection and review share that budget. Prioritise the brief's essential questions and return a concise supported delivery promptly; preserve secondary questions as unresolved instead of consuming all remaining time on them.

Optional guidance contains questions and grouping hints. It is neither evidence nor an organisational requirement. Without it, work from the assigned problem. Supplied statements may be incomplete or wrong; reconcile conflicts instead of assuming the earliest statement wins.

The host verifies every quoted passage from its own retrieval of the page, which covers the first part of a long page and is extended only when a passage is not found there; a site that splits a document into parts gives the host only the part at the cited address. Quote from what you actually read, cite the address of the part that holds the passage, and keep each finding inside what that part states; a conclusion that rests on text beyond the cited part is a lead, not a finding.

Prefer official and primary sources. Read the pages you cite. Each finding describes a sourced fact or a practice: kind fact states what the source reports; kind norm describes a practice the source recommends, not a rule adopted by the target organisation. Include applicability and limits. Methods, conditional judgement and historical cases are appropriate when needed by the assignment. Distinguish a documented incident from a worked example or an invented persona.

For each finding, name the question answered, source URLs and one exact passage from a source, with no additions or omissions. Quote matching verifies provenance, not whether the passage supports your conclusion. Describe substantive conflicts with supplied known information in words, without inventing material identifiers. Keep conclusions within what the sources support.

Write findings and the synthesis as a working memo to the person who commissioned the brief: the finding's text is a practitioner's statement of what holds and under what conditions, and the synthesis says what this means for the work, where the sources disagree and what remains unknown. Include a synthesis explaining supported conclusions, limits and your own proposed transfers. Record source disagreements or differences of purpose in disagreements; do not fabricate a conflict. Finding-level conflicts describe tensions with supplied known information. Unverified product examples and hypotheses are research directions, not evidence. Refer to sources by name or URL in the synthesis, not positional labels such as F1-F8 that may become misleading when findings are refused.

Every source-backed claim in the synthesis, working rules and disagreements must be supported by the submitted findings and their listed source pages. Extra readings or reader-generated summaries that are not included there remain investigation leads; do not silently use them as evidence. Mark your own transfers as unvalidated proposals at the point of use, not only in a distant limitations paragraph. A study that did not examine a variable does not establish that the variable is irrelevant.

On revision, admitted_findings were already accepted as evidence and need not be delivered again; deliver the repaired findings and the corrected synthesis. When revision.editorial is true, do not search or read anything new: the supplied source_views are the only material, the only acceptable repairs are rewording, narrowing, removing or re-quoting from those views, and any claim that would need another page stays an unanswered question. Reopening the same page with your own tools is not a repair. Keep that page as an explicitly unverified lead instead of asserting that it is now verified. When a supplied page contains the passage with different formatting, correct the quotation from that view. Do not claim host acceptance in your search log; describe your own reads separately from independent verification.
